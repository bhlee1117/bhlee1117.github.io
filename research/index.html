Research

Research interests

I study how neural activity shapes molecular responses during learning, using optical tools to read out activity and molecules in the same neurons of behaving animals.

Questions

From activity patterns to molecular programs

  1. 1

    How do neurons determine molecular programs in response to neural activity, such as plateau potentials, and how do these programs shape subsequent neuronal function?

  2. 2

    How do learning rules that shape neural activity influence molecular responses?

Research experience

Seeing activity and molecules in the living brain

01

In vivo imaging of Arc mRNA following memory formation

Endogenous Arc mRNA is tagged with RNA stem-loop repeats (Arc-PBS × PCP-GFP mice), so nascent transcripts light up as transcription sites in hippocampal neurons of living mice. Pairing this with two-photon Ca2+ imaging during virtual-reality behavior compares the activity of Arc+ and Arc− neurons.

Lee, Shim et al., PNAS 2022 →

02

All-optical dendritic electrophysiology in behaving mice

Combining the optogenetic actuator CheRiff with the voltage indicator Voltron2 lets me stimulate neurons with light and record voltage simultaneously across the dendritic tree — basal, soma, trunk, oblique and distal branches — of hippocampal pyramidal neurons in awake mice.

Lee et al., bioRxiv 2026 →

Future program

Three directions

01

AI-assisted programmable microscopy

Programmable microscopy enables (i) adaptive imaging, (ii) real-time analysis and intervention, (iii) molecular tool screening, and (iv) closed-loop neural interfaces.

02

Linking activity patterns to gene expression

Combine (i) population voltage imaging, (ii) phototagging, and (iii) transcriptomic profiling to identify molecular responses associated with neural activity and build predictive models of activity-dependent gene expression.

03

Shaping molecular responses through BCI

Use operant closed-loop reinforcement to train specific neural activity patterns and test whether different patterns produce distinct molecular responses.
