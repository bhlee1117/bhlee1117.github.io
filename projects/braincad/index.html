BrainCAD

3D stereotaxic planning for injections, implants and optical access

A browser workspace that puts the mouse brain atlas and the hardware of an experiment into the same 3D scene, so a surgery can be checked before it is attempted.

Open BrainCADGitHub

Why I built it

Planning a prism implant means holding several things in your head at once: where the target sits in the atlas, what angle the objective needs, and whether the implant can get there without hitting the headplate or another implant. I was doing that with paper sketches and a lot of guessing.

BrainCAD makes the question literal. Place the target, place the hardware, and the geometry answers for you.

What it does

Enter a stereotaxic coordinate and it tells you which structure you are in, with the three orthogonal atlas slices updating around the target. Pipettes, cannulas, prisms, objectives and imported STL/OBJ/GLB parts go into the same scene, with clearance and collision checks between them.

It renders the view down an objective, overlays axon projection data from the Allen connectivity atlas, and can run the connectivity question backwards: given a coordinate I can physically reach, what projects to it?

A plan saves to a file and exports as a planning sheet with fixed viewpoints, so two plans can be compared side by side.

Decisions worth noting

Bregma positions and atlas-to-stereotaxic scalings are not hard-coded. Each coordinate profile carries its citation and its confidence, and the app refuses to pair a profile with a volume it was not measured on, because that mistake produces coordinates that look fine and are wrong by millimetres.

The atlas is an averaged brain, not the skull in front of you, so collision checking runs between objects rather than against tissue by default. Clearance beyond the exact-query window is reported as a labelled lower bound rather than a precise-looking number that is not.

Tests run against the real downloaded atlas and assert that published coordinates land in the structures they were published for. Self-consistency tests cannot catch an axis swap; these can, and did.
